        Proof of concept, partly mock. The Publications and OpenAIRE tabs show real figures from static snapshots of the ORI DuckLake and the OpenAIRE APIs. The Persons, Organisations and Projects tabs are mock-ups with made-up numbers, for inspiration only: those datasets are not in the DuckLake. What can be measured, and how, is in Plan & feasibility.

        Findings so far

        • Only publications are in the ORI DuckLake. The cris schema has one table, publications (2.4 M records from 18 institutions). There are no CERIF Persons, OrgUnits or Projects, so those three tabs can only be mock-ups for now.
        • The harvested publications carry no ORCID, no ROR per affiliation and no corresponding author. Authors and affiliations only have an internal ID and a name; linked projects only an internal ID and a title, so there is no grant number or funder either.
        • That is how the CRIS export is built, not something the harvest drops: in the CERIF-XML that the Pure endpoints of UvA, UU and RUG deliver for publications, an author is only a reference (<cerif:Person id="…">). The ORCIDs are in the separate Person records (set openaire_cris_persons; 29 of the first 100 at UvA, 7 at UU). Every Pure CRIS endpoint checked offers the persons, orgunits, projects and funding sets, but the DuckLake only harvests publications — harvesting the other sets would make ORCID (and project identifiers) measurable.
        • Measured: DOI, abstract, open access status and linked project, per institution, publication year and publication type.
        • OAI-PMH sets (checked on the endpoints, 3 Nov 2026): the OpenAIRE CRIS guidelines make all nine openaire_cris_* sets mandatory. 16 of the 18 reachable CRIS endpoints recognise all nine (Radboud’s Metis misses funding, patents, events and equipments), but many are empty: equipments everywhere but Twente, funding and projects at most institutions, and Leiden’s endpoint has no records in any set. OpenAIRE itself harvests only publications, products and patents from these endpoints — persons and orgunits at one, projects and funding at none.
        • OpenAIRE: 89 of the 91 RPOs in the nl-orgs baseline are known to OpenAIRE (matched on ROR), with 183 datasources; 11 RPOs have a CRIS registered as compatible with the OpenAIRE CRIS guidelines. The OpenAIRE result tables in the DuckLake have no links to datasources or organisations (collectedfrom, organizations and projects are empty), so the counts per datasource come from the OpenAIRE Graph API instead.

        Plan

        1. Discover the schema — done with probe/probe.py.
        2. Count presence and validity for the fields that are there — done with probe/extract.py, which also embeds the snapshot in this page.
        3. Find out where ORCID, ROR and grant IDs get lost: compare a few records’ CERIF-XML straight from an institution’s OAI-PMH endpoint with the harvested row.
        4. Persons, organisations, projects: ask SURF to harvest the CERIF Person, OrgUnit and Project sets (OpenAIRE Guidelines for CRIS Managers) into the DuckLake. Until then, openaire.organizations, openaire.projects, openalex.awards and nl-orgs.baseline can serve as comparison sources, not as substitutes.
        5. Check the OAI-PMH endpoints themselves — done with probe/oai_check.py on the 82 base URLs in probe/oai-urls.json (38 reachable; plain-http endpoints could not be reached from the check environment).
        6. Ask SURF to harvest the persons, orgunits and projects sets that the CRIS endpoints already offer, so ORCID, organisations and projects can be measured with the same checks.
        7. Refresh the snapshots regularly with probe/extract.py and probe/openaire.py.

        How it was tested

        • The validity checks are DuckDB macros in notebooks/02-metadata-quality/probe/checks.sql in the dashboards repo, with 29 passing test cases in probe/test_checks.py: the ORCID and ISNI ISO 7064 MOD 11-2 check digit, DOI prefix and whitespace errors (and old SICI DOIs, which are valid), ROR, COAR access rights, Crossref Funder IDs, RAiD and ISO dates.
        • The schema and the counts come from a direct DuckDB connection to the public ORI DuckLake catalog on 3 November 2026.

        About the DURF Metadata Monitor

        DURF Theme 2: Metadata Quality Improvement — proof of concept

        This page sketches a monitor for DURF Theme 2 – Metadata Quality Improvement (see the DURF Gantt chart), as a companion to the DURF Full Text Monitor. Its purpose is to give evidence for how incomplete the metadata in Dutch CRIS systems is: how many records carry a DOI, an ORCID, a ROR, an abstract, a grant ID, an open access status and a corresponding author — and, when a field is filled in, whether its value is actually well formed.

        Partly mock: the Publications tab is a real, static snapshot of the ORI DuckLake. The Persons, Organisations and Projects tabs are generated at random in your browser, because those datasets are not in the DuckLake; they only illustrate layout and possible charts.

        Three states per field

        • Valid — filled in and passes the check (format, check digit, controlled vocabulary).
        • Invalid — filled in, but fails the check (e.g. a DOI stored as a URL, an ORCID with a wrong check digit).
        • Empty — the field exists in the harvested records but is not filled in.
        • Not in the harvested records — the field does not occur in the harvested CERIF at all (ORCID, ROR, corresponding author).

        Source: SURF ORI DuckLake, table cris.publications, harvested via OAI-PMH (CERIF-XML, OpenAIRE profile) from Dutch institutional repositories and CRIS systems. Static snapshot: changes after the extraction date are not included.
